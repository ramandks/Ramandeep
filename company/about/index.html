Our story

We started because pilots kept dying

Orbo Flow was founded by a team who had spent years watching promising AI projects stall between demo and production. The models were rarely the problem. Integration, ownership, governance and the unglamorous work of exception handling were.

So we built the company around the other 90% — the parts that make an AI system something a business can rely on at 2am, not just something impressive in a boardroom. Today we run agents, automations and data platforms for organisations from ten-person startups to national enterprises, across 23 industries and more than 30 countries.

We are headquartered in New Zealand, with delivery people across Asia-Pacific, Europe and North America. Being small and far away made us disciplined about two things: shipping something real quickly, and writing down how it works so the client is never dependent on us.

What we believe

Four positions that shape what we will and will not build.

Start where it is measurable

If we cannot baseline the process before we automate it, we cannot prove the result afterwards. We will push back on use cases that fail this test, even exciting ones.

Boring reliability beats clever demos

Retries, alerting, audit logs and rollback paths are not overhead. They are the difference between a system people trust and one they quietly stop using.

Capability should stay with the client

We document, train and hand over. A healthy engagement ends with your team confidently running what we built together.

Data residency is not a detail

You choose the region. We tell you every processor involved, in writing, before anything is signed.

How we work

Small teams, short cycles, written handover

Every engagement runs with a named delivery lead, a fortnightly demo against real data, and documentation written as we go rather than at the end.

  • A named lead who stays with you from discovery to handover
  • Fortnightly working demos, not status decks
  • Fixed-price build scopes agreed before work starts
  • Runbooks, architecture notes and training as standard
  • Security review and DPA before any data moves
